📜 The complete policy
A. Exarkie business allocation
Exarkie works through a mix of commission, consignment and outright-purchase supplier arrangements. The long-term goal is to gradually own selected inventory.

First calculate genuine profit after product costs and operating expenses. Identify supplier obligations, taxes, debts and other due commitments. Distinguish profit from available cash. When profit is distributable and working capital is sufficient, use these targets: Business safety reserve 20% · Stock Ownership Fund 30% · Growth and reinvestment 10% · Owner's draw 40% (total 100%).

Do not allocate money needed for supplier payments, business obligations or essential working capital. If the business cannot afford the allocation, report the shortfall and adjust. The Stock Ownership Fund stays separate from operating cash, supplier-owned inventory and personal savings, and exists to buy selected products outright.

B. Personal owner's draw allocation

Applied only to the approved owner's draw: Home expenses 40% · Tithe 10% · Charity 5% · Parents / Mom 5% · Savings 10% · Personal care & kids 10% · Miscellaneous 5% · Personal debt repayment 15%.

The 10% tithe is your settled Christian conviction and is not reduced without your instruction. If the draw cannot cover essential commitments, the shortfall is flagged — never invent money or borrow irresponsibly. Once personal debts are cleared, move the debt allocation toward savings, investments or approved goals.

C. Reporting

Monthly: revenue, product costs, expenses and profit · cash and supplier obligations · safety reserve · Stock Ownership Fund balance and purchases · growth spending · owner's draw planned vs actual · all eight personal categories · household expenses and debt · savings and investments · planned vs actual with corrective actions. Cash, profit, inventory and liabilities are always kept distinct.

D. Advice and scripture

Every financial decision: establish the facts, calculate the impact, weigh commitments and risks, set out the choices, give a clear recommendation. Principles: Proverbs 21:5 (diligent planning) · Proverbs 27:23 (know your state) · Luke 14:28 (count the cost) · Proverbs 3:9–10 and Malachi 3:10 (honour God, tithe) · 2 Corinthians 9:6–7 (willing giving) · 1 Timothy 5:8 (household) · Proverbs 22:7 (debt) · Colossians 3:23 (wholehearted work) · Philippians 4:11–12 (contentment).

Direct biblical instruction is different from practical financial judgement, and no guaranteed wealth is promised or specific divine instruction claimed for any individual decision.

🧭 How to repay a business loan well
  • Know every loan. Lender, balance, rate, instalment, deadline and penalties, all in one place. That is what this page keeps.
  • Repay from cash, not from supplier money. Instalments due this month come off before any profit is allocated, so the Financial Policy never promises money the loan needs.
  • Look 30, 60 and 90 days ahead before sending extra money to a loan, and keep your safety reserve intact rather than sending every spare shilling to the lender.
  • Pay on time, every time. Behind-schedule loans show amber or red here. If you may miss a payment, talk to the lender before the date and ask about a revised date or term.
  • With several loans: clear past-due and penalty-heavy ones first, then either the highest interest first (pays least interest) or the smallest balance first (quick wins, more motivation).
  • Check your cover. Business Split shows a debt-cover figure: monthly profit divided by monthly repayments. Above 1.0 means you can meet the payments; lenders like to see 1.25 or more.
  • Refinancing only helps if the total cost, including fees, falls, not just the monthly amount.
  • Scripture: Proverbs 22:7 (the borrower is servant to the lender) · Luke 14:28 (count the cost first) · Romans 13:8 (owe no one anything except love) · Psalm 37:21 (the righteous pays back). These guide attitude; the figures above are practical judgement.
Sources: Zenind — repaying business loans without straining cash flow · Capital One — debt service coverage ratio
